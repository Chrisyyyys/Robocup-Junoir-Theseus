# Theseus V3 in Autodesk Fusion

A 3D layout model of the V3 robot, built by script so it can be regenerated and checked. **Revision 4** (the structure, mounting and usability redesign in [2026-10-07-theseus-v3-mechanical-design.md](../2026-10-07-theseus-v3-mechanical-design.md)) is the current model. The revision 3 layout model it replaced is kept as `Theseus_V3_baseline.f3d` and `v3_model.py` (its README is [README_rev3.md](README_rev3.md)). All rev 4 numbers come from [v3_params4.py](../v3-checks/v3_params4.py), which is unit-tested by `test_v3_params4.py`. Units cm, frame x forward, y left, z up, origin at the midpoint of the drive axle on the floor line (Fusion is set to Z up here).

**Status:** layout model, revised on 9 Oct (spec section 0, rows 20 to 23: the chute numbers raised for reliability, the real ToF and camera boards with their mounts, the ring 5 mm taller, the camera cages, the front floor port and the IMU; rows 24 and 25, later the same day: the real omni wheel and its mount, the front floor port at y 3.5 and the antenna at -22 degrees). Every part sits at its specified position and every interface the spec defines is modelled. Nothing has been measured or printed; masses and the PCB are placeholders as in the spec. It does not replace the CAD detail design (see "Not modelled").

## Rebuild

With the Fusion MCP tools: one call of `fusion_mcp_execute` (`featureType: "script"`) per step, with this script (change the last line):

```python
import sys
sys.path.insert(0, r"<repo>\docs\superpowers\specs\v3-fusion")
import v4_run


def run(context):
    print(v4_run.stage('all', reset=True))
```

`reset=True` replaces every component in the open design (it also switches a Part design to hybrid). A whole build is about two to four minutes: build in groups (`['tub']`, `['drive', 'cartridges', 'omni', 'nub', 'bumpers']`, ...) if a call times out. `v4_run.stage(names, build=False)` only runs the probes. `v4_run.report('interference')` (and `envelope`, `belly`, `removal`, `access`, `omni`, `tof`, `camera`, `clearances`, `stepper`, `mass`) runs one whole-model report. `v4_run.selftest()` checks the probe API on a throw-away part.

Running it through the MCP, three things to know:

- **A tool call is cut off after about 60 s.** The Fusion server then answers "unavailable" until the script has finished, and the model is intact afterwards. The `removal` report is the slow one: run it as `report('removal', only=[0, 1, 2, 3])`, `only=[4]`, `only=[5]`, and the teardown as `only=[6], window=(group, 1, 10**6)` for each of its 12 groups (`results/v4_removal.txt` was compiled that way and says so in its header). `window` vouches only for the steps it names.
- **Screenshots: set the camera by script.** The preset view directions of the screenshot tool, and `adsk.core.ViewOrientations`, give a rolled view in this document. Set `cam.target`, `cam.eye` and `cam.upVector` on `vp.camera`, call `vp.fit()`, then take the screenshot with `direction: "current"` (iso: eye (220, 220, 190), up (0, 0, 1); top: eye (0, 0, 300), up (0, 1, 0); side: eye (0, -300, 8), up (0, 0, 1); underside: eye (0, 0, -300), up (0, -1, 0); target (0, 0, 8)). The screenshot tool saves the PNG in the session's tool-results folder (its path is in the answer): copy it into `views/`. `viewport.saveAsImageFile` also works but draws the grid and the ground shadow, and `ImageSaveOptions` does not exist in this Fusion build. For the top view call `v4_run.show(hide=('Lid', 'Handle bar'))` first and `v4_run.show()` after.
- **Do not save or close the document from a script.** The export `Theseus_V3_rev4.f3d` is a copy; whether the Fusion document goes into a project is the user's decision.

## Not modelled

Heat-set inserts and the frame screws (the ToF boards, camera boards and the IMU do have their own M2.5 screws modelled), snap-tab and L-slit details (the lid hook tongues; the hook bumps are separate bodies), flexing of the prongs and hooks (their forces are calculations, see the spec; the removal report ignores both), the spring's forward leg, the heat-set inserts of the pillar and ear (their holes are modelled at the screws' radius), the omni wheel's rollers and hub, bumper hinge sockets, hinges and return springs, the nub boss, the ToF and camera cables and their plugs, hopper snap tabs, battery strap slots, cables and connectors, fillets and rounded channel corners, the plate hub, the encoder board outline, the BNO055's chip and header, the holders of the floor sensors FP and SM, the main PCB (a placeholder block). Part sizes for the power switch, start button, LEDs and USB-C socket are placeholders; the snap tabs or thumbscrews that hold the dropper unit on its seats are not drawn. **Estimates in the model (spec 8.4, Figure 11):** the camera's lens axis, lens holder and lens height, the PCB thicknesses (1.6 mm) and the connector height of the ToF board.

## Checks

| Report | Pass criterion |
|---|---|
| per-stage probes | material (or none) at the listed points of each component; exactly one body in each hopper and channel (`ONE_BODY`: a cut that leaves a loose chip splits a part in two and no probe point sees it); no overlap of the new components with any other |
| `interference` | no pair of components overlaps (hidden stepper bay excluded) |
| `native_interference.report()` | Fusion's own analysis of every body against every other: only the expected overlaps (prong and hook-bump roots, bumper plates touching their switches), each under its limit |
| `native_interference.report_loose()` | the parts that touch no other part are exactly the known ones (pocket and hole parts with clearance, and parts whose holder is not modelled), with the distance to their nearest neighbour; a support that gets lost shows up as an unknown part |
| `envelope` | nothing beyond r 10.5 except the bumper plates (11.0) and their switches behind them; highest point 16.0, the victim LED on the bar (limit 25); omni front edge inside the wall |
| `belly` | only the known parts hang below z 3.5 |
| `removal` | lid, wheels, kit plate and N20, channels, the dropper unit, battery, frame group and GIGA leave along their paths without touching anything |
| `access` | with the lid and the dropper unit off, the GIGA, shield and battery are at least 70, 70 and 80 % open from above |
| `omni`, `tof`, `camera` | omni sweep without interference; no ToF or camera ray blocked by a robot part |
| `clearances` | the gaps the spec quotes are at least their smallest acceptable value |
| `kit_path.report()` | the kit cube falls to 2 mm above the floor slab and slides out of both channels without touching a part; the two controls (a 17.5 mm cube at the 17 mm slot, a 20 mm cube in the 19 mm bore) are blocked |
| `stepper` | the 28BYJ-48 bay is free once the N20 cartridge is out |
| `mass` | centre of mass and front-lift limit (warns below 1.0 m/s^2) |

## Findings of the rev 4 build

The whole build and the eleven reports were run in Fusion on 8 Oct; every probe and every report passed, and each number matched the emulator's prediction within the tolerances the plan set (distances 0.3 mm, volumes 2 %, centre of mass 0.05 cm, access 5 points). The chute was redesigned that day (the first items below) and the chutes rebuilt. **On 9 Oct the model was revised (spec section 0, rows 20 to 23): the chute numbers raised for reliability, the real ToF and camera boards with their mounts, the ring 5 mm taller, the camera cages, the front floor port and the IMU. The whole model was rebuilt in Fusion (20 stages) and every stage probe and all reports were run again. Later the same day the omni mount (rows 24 and 25) changed the tub, the omni parts, the floor port and the antenna, and the whole model was rebuilt and checked once more; `results/` holds that last run.** Nothing needed an entry in the `ALLOW` table of `v4_checks3d.py` (it is empty: no pair of the 57 components overlaps above 0.001 cm3). What is not a plain pass:

- **The omni mount (9 Oct) was first built on the emulator, which found four faults before any Fusion call**, each now a unit test of `test_v3_params4.py`: the M4 axle screw's head, first drawn 4 mm out of the wheel's inboard face, swept through the floor beside the opening (0.078 cm3) and would have forced the opening into the GIGA post H1's footprint, so the head and its washer now sit in the wheel's bore; the 7 mm head of the first M4 pivot screw reached 0.5 mm into the floor (0.005 cm3) and scraped it on the removal path, so the pivot became an M3 screw with a 5.5 mm head; the first new antenna place at -17.5 degrees overlapped the bumper's recess wall (0.036 cm3) and moved to -22 degrees against that wall; and the 25.6 mm wheel caught the frame boss at +12 degrees on the straight lift of the teardown (0.087 cm3), so the removal path drops the wheel set after the axle screw is out. The stadium-shaped bay cuts of 8 Oct, extruded over the whole height, were replaced by two plan cuts through the floor (one of them removed nothing in the new layout, which makes Fusion raise). Then in Fusion: every probe of the 20 stages and all reports passed, the emulator's numbers held (mass 1286 against 1287 g, the same access shares, the same removal paths), and one clearance pair, the spring coil to the arm, was dropped from the report because the pivot tube inside the arm's bore touches it by design (distance 0). The teardown report now has 15 windows; groups 11 to 14 are the omni steps.

- **The first chute design failed a kit simulation and was redesigned** (the Kit path item has the story; spec 6.2). The redesigned chutes were first built on the emulator, which found the channel reaching 0.003 cm3 into the dropper floor (a cut at z 8.7 now trims the tube's top corners) and a tub probe that asked for wall where the bigger chute hole is; then in Fusion, where every probe passed and the stage check, both sets of reports and the kit path agreed.
- **The hopper's loose lintel** (only Fusion could see it): the bigger socket cut (21.2 mm wide, cut up to the channel's ceiling plane) left the hopper's downhill corner above the channel hanging free, an 85 mm3 piece that Fusion split off as a second body of each hopper (92 bodies in its native analysis instead of 90). The emulator keeps a body as a list of shapes and cannot see this. The socket tool is now 8 mm taller (`CHUTE['socket_up']`); `test_hopper_is_one_piece` (the convex model gridded at 0.5 mm: two pieces, 1.9131 and 0.0850 cm3, before the change) and the one-body rows of the chute stage check (`ONE_BODY`; with `socket_up` set to 0 both hoppers come out as two bodies in Fusion, so the check can fail) keep it from coming back.
- **Independent review of the redesign (8 Oct)**: a fresh reviewer on the working tree found seven things, each of which I checked by running it. The plate was missing from the simulation and its '1000 kits' were 100 poses shared by every friction value: the simulation now has the plate's pocket walls and a pose for every kit, and the 2 mm result went from 98.1 to 82.8 %. The bore floor had a notch where the floor slab ended 0.15 cm short of the trough cut (slab 2.5 to 2.8 cm; `test_channel_floor_has_no_hole` failed first, and the new floor probes of `v4_checks3d` fail in Fusion with the shorter slab, one side of each chute). '16 mm passes the cube in any attitude' was too strong (a corner stand needs 16.25 mm: `cube_slot.py`). The turning-room line of `kit_path.py` could no longer fail. The bore's margin of 0.16 mm over the cube's space diagonal is thinner than the kit's tolerance (spec 6.2, D21). The rebound at damping 0.3 is 0.45, not the 0.37 of the textbook formula. And `plate_tolerance` was still run for the 14.5 mm slot. Spec 6.2 and 12 have the details.
- **A second interference check, Fusion's own analysis, finds the same and a little more** (`native_interference.py`, `results/v4_interference_native.txt`; added after the plan's reports, because the plan's check compares components and ignores overlaps below 0.001 cm3). It compares every body with every other body and lists 10 overlapping pairs, all expected: the four cradle prongs (0.126 cm3 each: the root is sunk 0.5 mm into the floor and the outer end sits inside the cradle ledge) and the four lid hook bumps (0.0098 cm3 each: the outer face is sunk 0.1 to 0.2 mm into the skirt) are separate bodies only because they flex and are one printed part with the tub and the lid; the two bumper plates touch their microswitches by 0.00035 cm3 each, as in the rev 3 model. No other pair of parts overlaps. I checked the first two kinds by probing points inside both bodies, not only by their volumes.
- **Five parts touch nothing in the model** (`report_loose()`, `results/v4_loose_parts.txt`; each of the other 52 touches at least one other part): the front floor port FP and the silver module SM (0.50 mm from the tub shell: in their floor holes with clearance, the holders are not designed), the USB-C socket (0.50 mm: in its wall hole), the rear nub (2.11 mm short of the tub; its boss is not modelled) and the Wi-Fi antenna (0.05 mm off the wall, on purpose, it is stuck on). The nine ToF boards and the two camera boards are held since 9 Oct (screw posts, cages). These are modelling choices at layout level, not placement errors, but they are what the CAD detail design has to hold.
- **Kit path** (`kit_path.py`, `results/v4_kit_path.txt`, added after the plan's reports because delivering kits is the robot's main job): a 10.3 mm cube falls from its plate pocket through the 17 mm slot and the hopper to 2 mm above the channel's floor slab and slides down each 19 mm channel to 2 cm past the exit, centred and shifted 3 mm to each side and 2.5 mm up and down, without touching any part; the two controls (a 17.5 mm cube at the slot, a 20 mm cube in the bore) are blocked, so the check can see a blockage. The check is static: it cannot tell whether a kit tumbles into the channel. **A physics simulation did, and it failed the first design** (14.5 mm slot, 13 mm bore; MuJoCo on this geometry, `v3-checks/chute_dynamics.py`): kits within 10 degrees of the slot square did not get out in 10 of 10 trials, and widening the bore to 14.7 mm alone lifted them only to 30 %. The chute was redesigned on 8 Oct (14 mm pockets, 16 mm slot and hopper void, 18 mm bore, an open trough with a floor slab): 100 % of 1000 kits with the plate parked within 1 mm and with bouncy impacts, 82.8 % with it up to 2 mm off. **On 9 Oct the numbers were raised for reliability (13 mm pockets, 17 mm slot and void, 19 mm bore; spec 6.2, D21, D22):** `chute_dynamics`, with the plate's pocket walls in it, takes 100 % of 1000 kits out with the plate parked exactly, up to 1 mm off, with bouncy impacts and in four tolerance corners, 100 of 100 at 1.5 mm and 99.9 % of 1000 up to 2.0 mm, the allowance (slot - pocket) / 2; its geometry agrees with the rebuilt Fusion bodies at 7000 random points (none differs) and 8000 points at the faces (none on the wrong side; `chute_geometry_check.py`, `chute_samples.py`). Friction, kit mass and impact softness are assumed: the chute bench test (spec section 12) decides. My first version of the drop test kept the cube turned like the slot all the way down and reported an overlap with the channel walls where the kit has to be in the bore anyway; it now stops 2 mm above the floor slab, and its second line checks that the slot and the bore are wider than the cube's face diagonal.
- **Belly line (z 3.5): twelve known parts hang below it**, all by design: both wheels and the omni wheel (z 0.00), the rear nub (2.00), the omni arm (2.40) and its axle set (2.65), both drive motors (3.00), both chutes' lower lips (2.91; the spec's `chute_exit` calculation for the 19 mm channel says 2.77 because it runs the tube out to the body radius, while the model's tube ends flat 0.8 cm past the axis's exit point and so stops 2 mm inside the wall at its lower inner corner; the terrain tables use the lower 2.67; all three are on the safe side), the floor sensors FP and SM (2.80). Since the 9 Oct omni mount the pivot hardware stays above the line: the pivot tube's bottom is z 3.95 (4.5 mm above it) and the spring coil's z 3.62 (1.2 mm above it); the 4 mm pin of 8 Oct, which reached z 3.40, is gone.
- **The GIGA's BOOT0 button PB2 is covered by the ring** (known limit, spec 7.4: double-tap reset enters the bootloader). The reset button PB1 and the USB-C connector J12 are open from above with the lid off; the `access` report lists all three so that a change which hides PB1 or uncovers PB2 shows up.
- **The omni spring's legs (10 Oct):** the model got the forward spring leg (a wire along the arm, 4 degrees up from it, in the arm's component because it turns with it), the 2 mm seat pin it rests on, the printed 7.4 mm arbor under the coil, a straight rear leg on an M3 x 6 adjuster bolt (the tail is 8.5 mm wide now) and a 5.05 mm bore in the arm. The emulator passed first time (91 unit tests, the omni stage 41 probes), and Fusion agreed: all 20 stages pass, 57 components (193 bodies) without an overlap, Fusion's native analysis finds the ten joints of before and the forward leg lying on its pin (a line contact of no volume, listed in `EXPECTED` of `native_interference.py`), the omni sweep (now **11** moving bodies: the wheel set, the arm with its pin and leg, the axle screw set) is free at 13 positions, the teardown's 15 windows and the six other removal paths are free, mass 1287 g. The new report `report('legs')` measures the leg and pin against the stop screw, the wheel's rim, the coil and the adjuster head body by body (`LEG_PAIRS`, `results/v4_legs.txt`): leg over the stop screw 1.07 mm, pin to the screw 1.26 mm, leg tip to the rim 4.32 mm, pin to the rim 4.23 mm, arbor to the coil 0.50 mm, rear leg on the adjuster head 0.00 mm (by design). One thing the sweep taught: a body that turns with the arm has to be in a moving component, or the sweep finds it overlapping the pin it rests on at every other position; the leg is in `Omni arm` for that reason.
- **Access from above:** with the lid off the GIGA is 53 %, the main PCB 53 % and the battery 66 % open; with the lid and the dropper unit lifted out 84, 86 and 80 % (criteria 70, 70, 80 %; on 8 Oct the battery had 91 %: the camera boards and cages now reach over its outboard strip, and 80 % is exactly the limit). The silver module SM (49 %) and the two drive motors (left 0 %, 54 % with the unit out; right 0 %) are under the frame: the report prints them as "frame off" and does not judge them; the removal teardown covers them.
- **Removal paths:** all seven pass, with the lid hook bumps and the cradle prongs treated as flexing parts (`FLEX` in `v4_checks3d.py`; their bending is a calculation in the spec). The battery leaves only by sliding 4.5 cm back and 2 cm inboard before lifting, because Camera L and the ring corner are over it and the bridge with its power switch is in front of it. The report was run in pieces because of the 60 s tool limit (see Rebuild).
- **Spec clearances: all 29 are above their smallest acceptable value (9 Oct, with the omni mount: wheel to the tub 1.50 mm, arm to battery 4.36 mm, antenna to wheel 25.2 mm, to the bumper switch 8.65 mm and to the USB-C socket 6.10 mm; the 24 below are those of the earlier 9 Oct run)** (ten more than on 8 Oct: the camera boards and cages against the lid, wheel, battery, GIGA and kit plate, the IMU against the N20 and hopper A, the front port against the omni arm and the GIGA posts). Two spec numbers moved with the chute: chute to wheel 5.12 mm with the 19 mm channel (rev 3 quoted 6.7, the first rev 4 chute had 8.24, the 18 mm one 5.62; limit 5.0) and stepper bay to hopper B 3.97 mm (5.2 with the first hopper). Two are larger in 3D than the number the spec quotes (safe side, spec numbers left as they are): N20 to the left drive motor 10.86 mm (first model 10.6), stepper bay to the GIGA stack 5.76 mm (spec 5.0, a plan-view figure; 3D is larger where the heights differ). The tightest are the battery to the left motor (1.10 mm, spec 1.0), the GIGA stack to the tub (1.72 mm, spec 1.7), the camera cage to the lid (2.07 mm, limit 1.5), the omni wheel to the GIGA post H1 (2.87 mm, spec 2.9; the front floor port to the omni arm is 5.05 mm), the silver module SM to H1 (2.78 mm, placeholder module size) and the camera board to the lid hump (2.89 mm, limit 1.5; it rests on the estimated lens height, spec 8.4).
- **Mass 1287 g [placeholder masses]** (printed parts from the model volume at 45 % fill and 1.27 g/cm3, bought parts and the unmodelled wiring and fasteners from the rev 3 budget in `com.py`, whose total was 1070 g; the emulator had 1286 g; the 58 g more than in the earlier 9 Oct model are the real omni module: 94 g against 36.5 g). Centre of mass 1.09 cm ahead of the axle, 0.17 cm to the left, 6.79 cm high; front load 15.9 %; front lift-off limit **1.58 m/s^2** (rev 3: 1.5; 1.21 before the real omni wheel, whose extra mass sits far ahead of the axle), above the 1.0 the firmware's PWM ramp assumes and above the 1.2 a 0.25 s ramp to 0.3 m/s needs; still use a 0.3 s ramp. Weigh the printed parts: the limit moves with them.
- **Envelope:** the bumper plates reach 11.000 (their switches 10.752), everything else 10.5 at most; the USB-C socket's face is at 10.470 and the Wi-Fi antenna at about 10.15 (on the bumper recess wall, inner face 10.15), inside the wall's 10.3 inner face; the ToF modules reach 10.28 (screw tips inside their posts). The lid's camera humps were narrowed so that their corners stay inside 10.5: Fusion's mesh vertices found one at 10.512 that the emulator's sampling had missed. The highest point is the victim LED on the handle bar at z 16.0 (bar top 15.6, limit 25).
- **Emulator against Fusion:** the dry run's prediction table (plan, Task 9) held for all eleven reports on 8 Oct; on 9 Oct the emulator (`dryrun.py --repo`, five minutes, no clearance report) predicted Fusion's numbers again (mass 1228 against 1229 g, centre of mass 0.86 cm and 6.96 cm in both, identical access shares, no blocked ray, 54 components without an overlap), and again after the omni mount (mass 1286 against 1287 g, centre of mass 1.09 cm and 6.79 cm in both, 57 components, the same access shares and removal paths). What only Fusion showed: on 8 Oct the hopper's loose lintel (first items of the findings); on 9 Oct the lid hump's corner at r 10.512 (the emulator samples points, Fusion's mesh has the vertices) and a set of overlaps of 0.2 mm between camera parts that my first camera model made on purpose (holder, barrel, SD socket and screw shafts), which the native analysis counts as overlaps: those parts now touch with exactly coincident faces. The clearance report takes more than an hour on the emulator with the camera cages, so the 9 Oct clearances are Fusion's alone. What only Fusion showed about the first build was about the tool, not the model: the 60 s tool limit and the rolled preset views (both in Rebuild).
