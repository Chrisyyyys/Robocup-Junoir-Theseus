Numbers carry a tag: **[calc]** my arithmetic or an emulator run (`balance` and `wire_runs` are scripts in [v3-fusion/dryrun](v3-fusion/dryrun/README.md), `access` and `removal` reports of the rebuild plan, output in `dryrun/results/dryrun_full.txt`), **[check]** a script in [v3-checks/](v3-checks/README.md) (named; raw output in `v3-checks/results/`), **[src]** a vendor page or datasheet, **[proposal]** a design choice made here, **[measure]** to be measured on the real part, **[estimate]** read from a photo or inferred, to be measured, **[placeholder]** a guess.

This document extends [the rev 3 spec](2026-10-06-theseus-v3-robot-design.md), which stays the source for everything not changed here. It answers the review of the first Fusion model: chute and plate, dropper support, lid and electronics access, mounting holes and attachments, the omni position, clip-on motors, and the feasibility and usability of all of it. The rebuild plan was dry-run on a geometry emulator before any Fusion call ([v3-fusion/dryrun](v3-fusion/dryrun/README.md)); what it found is in row 15. Where this file disagrees with rev 3 sections 4 (omni), 7 (chutes, dropper motor) or 9 (electronics mounting), this file wins.

## 2. Assumptions and constraints

- **Printer:** bed size not measured; I assume a 220 x 220 mm class bed. The shell is 21.0 cm across, so the tub, the frame and the lid are each one print about 210 mm across. If the real bed is smaller they split along y = 0 (section 10). **[measure]**
- **Material:** PETG for everything that flexes and for the frame; PLA for rigid blocks. FDM tolerance about +-0.2 mm: sliding fits 0.2 to 0.3 mm clearance. **[proposal]**
- **Fasteners:** M3 heat-set inserts (5.7 mm long) in printed bosses at least 8 mm across, M3 screws for the frame and the camera cages, M2.5 x 6 for the motor face plates, the ToF boards, the camera boards and the IMU (self-tapping into 2.0 to 2.2 mm printed pilots). A 2.5 mm and a 2 mm hex key and a small wrench are the only tools. **[proposal]**
- **Masses:** rev 3 uses a placeholder 1.07 kg (`com.py`: printed chassis 150 g, lid plus handle 45 g, dropper plate and floor 30 g, chutes 15 g). A 21 cm PETG tub, ring frame, lid, handle and hoppers will weigh more, perhaps 0.2 to 0.3 kg extra **[placeholder: weigh the printed parts]**; the spring and screw loads below scale with the real mass. The rev 4 Fusion model sums to 1287 g with these placeholders and the real omni module (section 4).
- **Delegated on 7 Oct:** the omni arm, the dropper-motor question, the handle, the lid and the wheel arches were left to this review (D9 to D14, section 13). The print-bed size was left for you to measure.

### 4.3 The spring

A **torsion spring on a printed arbor on the pivot tube**: music wire 1.6 mm, mean coil 10 mm (OD 11.6, ID 8.4), **right-hand, 4.07 body turns** (4.29 active turns with the two legs), close-wound length 8.1 mm, in the 9.3 mm between the pillar and the arm. The 9 Oct version of this section left the legs half designed (the forward leg 'hooked on the arm's top edge', the rear leg bent to reach its bolt, the coil hanging on the tube); on 10 Oct they were made concrete **[calc]** `omni_mount` (output [results/omni_mount.txt](v3-checks/results/omni_mount.txt)); [Figure 14](v3-figures/fig14_omni_spring.png) is the order sheet. Both legs leave the top of the coil and are held **from below**:

- The **rear leg** (10 mm to its load point, 12 mm in all) is straight and lies on the hex head of an **M3 x 6 adjuster bolt** in an M3 x 5.7 insert in the pillar's tail, directly under it (the tail is 8.5 mm wide in y for that; the 9 Oct leg was bent 90 degrees to reach a bolt 3.3 mm inboard of its plane). The bolt is turned with a 5.5 mm socket from the side: **one turn is 2.9 degrees, 0.55 N at the axle**.
- The **forward leg** (11 mm to its load point, 12 mm in all) is straight and lies **along the arm, splayed 4 degrees up from it, on a 2 mm stainless pin in the arm**, 11 mm from the pivot. The coil ends 0.7 mm short of the arm's face, so the leg that continues its last turn lies beside the arm, not over it: a tangent leg is 5 mm from the pivot, its 1.6 mm wire spans 4.2 to 5.8 mm, the arm's top edge is at 6 mm, and the pin, standing 3.5 mm out of the arm's inboard face, is the arm's way of reaching the leg. The leg turns with the arm, so it never slides on the pin. The 4 degrees lift it to **1.07 mm over the stop screw** (parallel to the arm it passed 0.5 mm over it); cutting the slot 5 degrees low instead put the screw's head 0.4 mm into the floor on its pull-out path. The pin leaves 1.0 mm of bar over its hole and 1.1 mm to the slot's end.
- Because both supports are under the legs, the load **opens** the coil: 12.4 degrees at rest, 44.0 at full travel, the mean diameter growing from 10.0 to 10.3 mm. Winding it up instead would put the rear leg at the bottom of the coil, on the belly line (z 3.6). Opening is the less favourable direction for a spring (the tension is on the inner fibre, where the textbook factor 1.135 is already counted in the 1055 MPa below).
- The coil sits on a **printed arbor** 7.4 mm in diameter on the tube, between the pillar and the arm, with 0.5 mm of air to the free coil. Without it the 8.4 mm coil hangs on the 5 mm tube 1.7 mm off-centre, pushed up by its two supports until its top is at z 4.95, 0.5 mm over the height of the battery tray's underside (the tray does not reach this far in y), and the legs move with it.

48 % of the wire's strength, with the load opening the coil, is borderline for a spring that cycles every time the robot crosses a step. By the usual textbook fatigue limits for unpeened music wire (of the order of half the tensile strength for repeated loading from zero, at a million cycles) the stress range of 753 MPa is inside the limit, but the bends at the leg roots carry a stress factor of 1.3 or more, which leaves it little margin; at 10^4 to 10^5 strokes (a season of testing, an estimate) the limits are higher **[estimate from the textbook limits, not a test]**. So **run a life test of the spring alone** (10 000 cycles between the two torques of the order sheet; backlog O5), ask the supplier for stress relief and, if offered, shot peening, and order three. The fallback is a compression spring 25 mm from the pivot (4.3 N preload, 15.0 N at full travel, wire 0.8 mm, OD 9.7 mm, about 9 active coils, 31 mm free length); it does not fit above the arm, where the strip between the wheel's outer face (y 1.58) and the battery's near edge (y 2.2) is 6 mm wide, so it would have to lie along the arm.

### 4.4 Stops, loads and strength

**The stop is a fixed M3 x 25 screw** from the pillar (head on its inboard face), across the corridor, **through an arc slot in the arm** and into a 2.6 mm pilot hole in the ear (7.5 mm of thread), 8 mm ahead of the pivot: it clears the coil's 5.8 mm radius by 0.7 mm. The slot is an arc of radius 8 mm about the pivot, 3.3 mm wide with round ends, 0.2 mm longer than the screw's travel at each end; its two ends are the **rest stop** and the **compression stop**. 2.1 mm of bar is left above and below it and 3.8 mm to the pivot bore. The first draft had a pin on the arm running in a slot of the pillar, 7 mm from the pivot: the pin would have grazed the coil and, cantilevered 11 mm from the arm, would have carried the stop load at about 600 MPa in an M3 screw; the fixed screw is held at both ends (16.5 mm between its supports) and the stop load goes into the aluminium slot end (the screw's threads bear on that aluminium: at rest the spring's 107 N.mm presses it with 13 N, about 170 MPa at the thread crests by the Hertz formula for a line contact, assuming 0.5 mm of crest contact along the 3 mm of the bar: elastic; in the 70 N impact case about 400 MPa, more than the aluminium yields at, so the first hard landings dent the slot end a little and the dent spreads the load **[estimate]**. A thin smooth sleeve over the screw (3.6 mm outside, as long as the pivot tube, the slot 3.9 mm wide) is the cure if the stair test shows the rest end creeping: it still fits, 0.4 mm from the coil and 0.8 mm from the forward leg; a 4 mm sleeve or a shoulder screw would not (backlog O8). It is not needed for the first robot). **Ride height** follows the slot end: 0.2 mm of error moves the wheel 1.1 mm, so the rest end is cut short and filed to the measured ride height (backlog O10).

### 4.6 Costs: statics, balance and the acceleration limit

**Statics** **[calc]** `omni_mount` (the 9 Oct model, 1.286 kg; `statics.py` keeps the rev 3 budget of 1.07 kg). The front load is **15.9 %** (rev 3: 11.9 %, 8 Oct: 13.6 % at the 1.07 kg budget). A 2 cm step pushed over by the drive wheels needs 0.45 W against 1.26 W of traction (margin x2.8, was x3.4; 1 cm: x7.1); the torque per wheel for a 2 cm riser is 1.87 kg.cm and for a 25 degree ramp 1.09 kg.cm (gearbox limit 5). Coming down a 25 degree ramp the front load is 62 percent, 7.8 N, which the spring (4.3) carries within the 2.5 cm travel. All of these scale with the real mass: the spring needs about 0.19 N/cm more for every 0.1 kg.

**Acceleration limit.** The emulator model of this layout (printed parts from the model volume at 45 % fill, bought parts from the rev 3 budget with the omni module at its bought-part masses, wiring and fasteners as unmodelled lumps) weighs **1286 g**, the Fusion model of the same layout 1287 g, with the centre of mass **1.09 cm ahead of the axle and 6.79 cm high**: front load 15.9 % (2.0 N on the omni), front lift-off at **1.58 m/s^2** (1.21 before the real wheel), nose-down tip when braking at 8.3 m/s^2 **[calc, placeholder masses]** `balance --repo` (9 Oct; output [dryrun/results/balance.txt](v3-fusion/dryrun/results/balance.txt), Fusion `v4_mass.txt`). The omni module weighs 94 g: wheel 73 g and its bearings, sleeve and spacer 77 g, arm 6 g, axle screw 3 g, pivot and stop screws 3 g, spring and its tube 4 g, adjuster 1 g; the 8 Oct budget had 36.5 g (a 30 g wheel and a printed arm). The 57 g of extra mass moves the centre of mass 0.23 cm forward and 0.17 cm down, which **raises the lift limit** (the omni is further ahead of the centre of mass than the extra weight is) and raises the front load by 3.6 points. The ramp margin is better than before: a 0.3 m/s top speed reached in 0.25 s needs 1.2 m/s^2, the limit is 1.58. Still use a PWM ramp of 0.3 s (1.0 m/s^2, what the rev 3 firmware assumed; D5) and weigh the printed parts.

**Axle position (second round: could the drive wheels move back?).** The drive train (wheels, motors, face plates, 302 g) moves with the axle and everything else stays in the body, so each centimetre back moves the centre of mass 0.75 cm forward relative to the axle. **[calc]** `balance` for the balance, **[check]** `axle_shift` for the terrain (same side-view simulator and cases as the omni study, the chute lip kept where the wheel needs it; re-run on 9 Oct with the real omni layout):

### 4.7 Service

**The wheel and its bearings come off from below without opening the body:** robot on its back, unscrew the axle screw from the inboard side until it leaves the arm (3.5 mm; a **ball-end 3 mm hex key at an angle**, because the silver module sits 1.6 cm inboard of the wheel at the same height), and the wheel set, with the screw still through its bearings, drops out of the opening. **The arm, spring and pivot** need the frame off (3.3): then the pivot and stop screws come out inboard (2.5 mm hex key; its elbow needs the column above y -1.7, which the GIGA stack covers, so the stack comes out first), the wheel set drops, and the arm with the spring on its tube lifts out (the forward leg lies on the arm's pin and the rear leg on the adjuster head, so both lift off their supports; the arbor and the coil stay on the tube, the pin in the arm). The whole path was run in Fusion as four steps of the teardown report. The wheel does not go straight up: at 25.6 mm it would catch the frame boss at +12 degrees (the first teardown check found it). Spring replacement and the preload (4.3) are done with the arm out.

## 5. Drive train

### 5.1 Motor cartridge and snap cradle

Requested: motors held by an open-sided snap cradle, not screwed to the robot. [Figure 8](v3-figures/fig8_motor_clip.png) and [Figure 9](v3-figures/fig9_mounting.png).

**A snap alone cannot hold the torque.** The motor body is a smooth 20 mm cylinder **[src]**. A hook that keeps a few N of preload on it gives a friction torque of only 0.04 to 0.16 kg.cm (two hooks, friction 0.35); a 2 cm riser needs 1.63 kg.cm and the gearbox limit is 5 **[check]** `mount_check`. The torque has to go through a key.

**Design.**
- **Cartridge:** the motor plus a 3 mm PETG **face plate** screwed to the gearbox face once (2 x M2.5 x 6: 3 mm of plate plus 3 mm of thread, inside the 3.5 mm limit **[src]**; hole spacing **[measure]**). The plate has a 4.4 mm bore for the D-shaft and **one** ear about 8 x 3 mm on the front (+x) side, 15.5 to 23.5 mm from the axle. One ear, not two: on the right motor a rear ear would run into the GIGA post H4 at (-2.27, -6.43).
- **Cradle (printed with the tub):** the seat is the floor cut to the motor radius (20.3 mm), so the lower flanks rest on the floor edge. An outer web 2.5 mm thick (y 6.45 to 6.7, 3 mm clear of the wheel hub at y 7.0, x -1.6 to 2.75) and a ledge each side of the motor (y 5.65 to 6.05; the front one x 1.06 to 2.75, the rear one only x -1.6 to -1.06) form the slot for the plate, 3.4 to 4.0 mm for the 3.0 mm plate, which also cuts through the floor. The ear sits in the slot beside the front ledge and stops the turning. At the gearbox limit the ear carries 25 N on 24 mm^2, 1.0 MPa **[calc]**.
- **Prongs:** two thin vertical walls, one each side of the motor, cantilevered along the axle from the ledges (free length 32 mm, so y 2.85 to 6.05), with a hook at the free end 5.5 mm above the axle and a 0.75 mm undercut. For 2.4 mm thick, 11 mm tall, 32 mm free length the hook must deflect 2.4 mm to pass the equator: 5.6 N to push in per prong, strain 0.84 %, about 4.6 N to pull out, friction 0.15 kg.cm. Across the options tried (1.6 to 2.9 mm thick, 32 to 40 mm free) push-in is 1.3 to 7.6 N, strain 0.4 to 1.0 %, pull-out 1 to 6 N **[check]** `mount_check` (PETG, E 2 GPa, uniform-thickness beam, wedge estimate for the pull-out; the real hook is thicker, so these are indicative).
- **Why walls along the axle:** a printed arm that stands up from the floor bends across the layers and cracks after a few cycles; a wall that bends sideways bends along them (section 10).
- **Retention is modest by nature:** the hook only keeps the cartridge seated against rebounds. If a drop test shows it popping out, add a small detent on each ear or a finger-tight thumbscrew through each plate ear. Release: spread the prongs with two fingers.

Print three cradles (hook height, undercut and wall thickness varied) and keep the one that needs a firm thumb push to seat and a deliberate spread to release. PLA is too brittle for a snap used many times.

**Encoder.** The encoder board's outline is not on the Pololu page. If it sticks out of the 20 mm silhouette, turn that side up or away from the GIGA post H2 at (-1.62, -1.62). If the face-plate screws are unwanted, the alternative is a notch that keys the encoder board, which needs its outline first. **[measure]**

### 5.2 Wheels, tyres and the arches

80 mm silicone tyres, 20 mm wide, on printed hubs on the 4 mm D-shaft. The shaft is 18 mm long **[src]**: 9 mm run from the gearbox face (y 6.1) across the 3 mm gap and the web to the hub face (y 7.0), and 9 mm engage the hub, with an M3 set screw on the flat. **The wheel cannot leave the robot as modelled:** to clear the shaft it must slide about 1 cm outwards, and its outer corner (r 9.85 at x +-4.0, y 9.0) would then reach r 10.7, past the wall's inner face at 10.3. You will swap tyres often (the stair test depends on friction), so the lower wall gets an **arch** on each side, x +-4.4, z 3.5 to 8.3, and the floor opening runs out to the wall: the wheel slides 1 cm along the shaft and drops out downwards. The ring above stays continuous; the lower wall loses about 9 cm of its circumference on each side and gets its stiffness from the floor and the ring **[proposal]**. A snap-in cover for the arch is possible if the openings prove a snag risk.

Two things follow from the arches (found when the rebuild plan was dry-run, **[calc]**). First, the 4 mm strip of wall above each arch (z 8.3 to 8.7) lies in the middle of the camera's field of view: rays from the lens tip (z 9.3, 1.7 cm inside the wall) at -22 to -30 degrees cross it, and the optical axis at -20 degrees just clips its top edge. It is **notched 3 cm wide at the camera window**, so the window is open from the arch up to z 10.6 over that width. Second, a frame boss must not hang over the tyre: at 101 degrees the boss underside (z 7.70) was 1 mm below the tyre's top rim (7.80) once the wheel slid out, and it would have hung from the thin strip. The rear screws are therefore at **+-118 degrees**, with the boss centre just outside the arch (x -4.6 against the arch edge at -4.4; above z 7.7 the tyre only spans x +-1.5), and every boss has a rib to the wall.

At x 8.0 the bump on a ramp falls to 0.19 and at 8.5 to 0.08, so 7.5 is about the limit; a face at z 3.3 would add 0.5 cm on the 30 degree stairs and 0.2 on the bump on a ramp **[check]** `fsens_front` (re-run on 9 Oct with the real omni layout; on 8 Oct: 2.37, 0.82, 0.50 and 0.17). ### 6.2 Hopper and chute

Rev 3: slot 14.5 mm square at 45 degrees, chute axis starting under the slot at z 7.95. In the first Fusion model the tube mouth was a tilted disc 0.75 cm below the floor and touched it along one edge only. The first rev 4 design (14.5 mm slot and hopper void over a 13 mm square channel, the trough cut flat at z 7.7) passed every static geometry check and then **failed a rigid-body simulation of a kit** (8 Oct). The redesign of 8 Oct (14 mm pockets, 16 mm slot and hopper void, 18 mm bore) passed it only for a plate parked within 1 mm, with 0.16 mm to spare in the bore. **On 9 Oct you asked for the design to be picked on reliability alone, so the numbers were raised to 13 mm pockets, a 17 mm slot and hopper void and a 19 mm bore (D21, D22)**; the design below is that one. What it costs is 0.5 mm of the gap from chute to wheel (5.12 mm, limit 5.0) and 0.5 mm of hopper wall; what it buys is in the bullets. An independent review of the 8 Oct redesign had found that the first simulation left the plate out and shared its poses between friction values; the simulation below has the plate's pocket walls and a pose of its own for every kit.

- **Slot, hopper void and bore:** slot 17.0 mm square (turned 45 degrees), hopper void 17.0 mm square, channel bore 19 x 19 mm inside with 1.6 mm walls (22.2 mm outside). A 10.3 mm cube has a 14.57 mm face diagonal and a 17.84 mm space diagonal. The 17 mm slot and void leave 2.43 mm over the face diagonal, so the cube passes lying at any turn, and tipped **any** way, even standing on a corner, which needs 16.25 mm (the 16 mm slot of 8 Oct failed the 0.9 % of random attitudes within 4.5 degrees of a corner stand, the 14.5 mm slot of the first design 33 % of them, since it is narrower than the face diagonal) **[check]** `cube_slot`, `mount_check`. The 19 mm bore is 1.16 mm wider than the space diagonal (0.81 mm for a 10.5 mm kit, the top of rev 3's 10.3 +-0.2 mm), so a kit that tumbles after its bounce cannot wedge between two side walls or between floor and ceiling, even with the printed hole 0.4 mm small: in the four tolerance corners (10.3 or 10.5 mm kit, bore 19.0 or printed 18.6 mm) and with a 6 g kit all 180 of 180 kits get out at both damping ratios **[check]** `chute_dynamics` (the 18 mm bore of 8 Oct lost 2 to 5 % of the kits with a 10.5 mm kit or a small bore). The square section is centred 1.5 mm above the axis (the inner floor is 8 mm below the axis, the ceiling 11 mm above), so the floor (inner 8 mm, outer 9.6 mm below the axis) is exactly where it was in the 18 mm channel and the lowest point at the wall does not change.
- **Hopper:** a box under each slot, 22 mm outside and 17 mm inside (2.5 mm walls), from z 7.2 up to the floor underside (z 8.7), with a 26 mm flange 1.5 mm thick sealed flat against the floor by two snap tabs. It is a separate part because it hangs below the print plane of the dropper floor, and it lifts out with the unit. Where the channel leaves it, the hopper has a socket: the channel's own section cut out of the downhill wall and floor strip, made 8 mm taller than the channel, so that no piece of the hopper is left hanging above the channel (cut only to the channel's ceiling it left a loose 85 mm3 lintel, and Fusion split each hopper into two bodies; `v4_checks3d` now checks one body per hopper and channel).
- **Open trough:** inside the hopper the channel has no walls and no ceiling. A 25 mm square cut (turned 45 degrees, from z 5.0 up) removes everything of the tube inside it, and a floor slab (outer width, one wall thick, 2.8 cm along the axis from the slot centre) is laid back, so that a kit lands on the inclined floor with no wall end, ledge or ceiling edge to catch it; the cut is bigger than the void so that it goes right through the side walls where the tube begins, and the walls end in a V that leads a kit into the bore. The slab has to reach past the point where the cut's vertical wall meets the sloping floor, 2.55 cm (top face) to 2.65 cm (underside) along the axis, not the 2.04 cm of the cut's corner at axis height: at 2.5 cm it left a notch in the bore floor (found by the review; `test_channel_floor_has_no_hole`, and floor probes in `v4_checks3d` that fail in Fusion with the shorter slab; the figures are those of the 18 mm channel, and the test scans the 19 mm channel's floor the same way with the slab at 2.8 cm and finds no hole). The tube's top corners just outside the cut would reach into the dropper floor, so they are cut at its underside (z 8.7). The hopper and the channel do not overlap, so the channel pulls out through the wall.
- **Axis:** slot centre (-4.73, +-2.73, 7.95) to the exit (-6.0, +-8.62, **4.15**), slope 32.2 degrees (rev 3: 34.9). A straight tube meets the cylindrical wall at about 22 degrees, so its lower edge runs further down the slope than the 2D end cap showed. - **Kit simulation of the final design (9 Oct)** **[check]** `chute_dynamics` (MuJoCo 3.15): a 1.2 g kit starts at rest on the floor in its plate pocket over slot B, falls through the slot and the hopper and slides down the channel to the exit. The geometry is the Fusion bodies of the 9 Oct model (`chute_geometry_check`: 0 of 7000 random points differ and none of 8000 points 0.3 mm either side of the faces is on the wrong side). The plate is the four walls of its 13 mm pocket, 5 mm thick, z 9.0 to 10.2, 0.3 mm above the floor, parked exactly over the slot or off along the ring (the pocket turns with the plate). Every kit has its own random turn (up to +-18 degrees, the most the 13 mm pocket allows) and place in the pocket. **Plate parked exactly: 100 % of 1000 kits get out** (friction 0.15 to 0.60, 100 kits each); **parked up to 1 mm off (1.5 degrees): 100 % of 1000**; **1.5 mm off: 100 of 100** at friction 0.35 and 0.55; **up to 2 mm off, the geometric allowance: 99.9 % of 1000**; with bouncy impacts (contact damping ratio 0.3 instead of 1: 0.45 of the impact speed comes back, against 0.13) and the plate parked exactly: **100 % of 1000**; in the four tolerance corners (kit 10.3 or 10.5 mm, bore 19.0 or printed 18.6 mm) and with a 6 g kit: **180 of 180 each**, at both damping ratios. The run fails unless its controls pass: the engine gives the textbook acceleration g (sin a - mu cos a) on a plane tilted 32.2 degrees (3.02, 2.28 and 1.45 m/s2 at friction 0.25, 0.35 and 0.45, against 3.15, 2.32 and 1.49 by the formula), holds a cube still at friction 0.70 (above tan 32.2 = 0.63), and stops an 18.5 mm cube at the 17 mm slot. The 3D kit-path check in Fusion (`v3-fusion/kit_path.py`) lets the cube fall from its pocket to 2 mm above the floor slab and slide out of both channels, centred and shifted 3 mm to each side and 2.5 mm up and down, without touching a part, and blocks a 17.5 mm cube at the slot and a 20 mm cube in the bore (`v3-fusion/results/v4_kit_path.txt`).
- **Parking error and the approach to the slot: the limits found** **[check]** `chute_dynamics`, `plate_tolerance`. *Parking.* A kit lying corner to corner in its pocket is as wide along the ring as the pocket (13 mm) and the slot is 17 mm, so the plate may be parked off by (17 - 13) / 2 = **2.0 mm** (3.0 degrees at the ring) before the kit's corner hangs over the slot's edge and the pocket wall pins it (8 Oct: 1.0 mm). The simulation agrees and shows how sharp the edge is: of 100 kits each, at friction 0.35 and 0.55, with the plate off by 0, 1.0 and 1.5 mm all 100 and 100 get out, by 2.0 mm 90 and 87, by 2.5 mm 57 and 61, by 3.0 mm 27 and 40; in the sweep up to 2.0 mm the kits that got out were, by how far off the plate was (mm), 0.0 to 0.5: 146 of 146; 0.5 to 1.0: 168 of 168; 1.0 to 1.5: 165 of 165; 1.5 to 2.0: 161 of 164 (87.9 % of 1000 in the sweep up to 3.0 mm). The clearance under the plate (0.1, 0.3 and 0.6 mm) made no difference. The other rule, that a neighbouring pocket must not reach the slot, is looser: its first overlap comes at about 4 degrees (`plate_tolerance`: park margin 1.92 mm, wall 4.06 mm, 128 cases, no violation). **The dropper has to park the plate within +-1.5 mm, aiming at +-0.5 mm**: approach the park position from the same side every time (the N20's gearbox has backlash) and measure the repeatability on the bench (the 8 Oct design needed +-1 mm; D22). *Approach.* A plate that turns the pocket onto the slot (from 20 degrees away) and stops there at once: 30 of 30 kits get out at 60, 120 and 200 degrees/s, at friction 0.15, 0.35 and 0.55, so the approach speed no longer matters **[assumed friction; a real stop is not instant]** (8 Oct: only 4 of 30 at 60 degrees/s and friction 0.55).
- **What the simulation does not know, and the corners of the tolerances:** friction (one Coulomb value for every printed surface, 0.15 to 0.60), the impact softness, a uniform cube (the real kit may be weighted off centre: bench test), the plate's hub, motor and the other pockets, the real plate speed and stop, the tub floor, wall and wheel (a kit that leaves anywhere but the exit counts as lost), and the plate's real clearance. The kit's mass is not decisive: a 6 g kit (the mass budget has 6 g per kit) does as well as the 1.2 g one. The bore has 1.16 mm to spare over the cube's space diagonal, more than the kit's own tolerance and a printed hole's shrinkage: a 10.5 mm kit (rev 3's 10.3 +-0.2 mm), a bore printed 0.4 mm small and both together all get out in 180 of 180, bouncy impacts or not (the 18 mm bore of 8 Oct, with 0.16 mm to spare, lost 2 to 5 % of them). The first version of this simulation (8 Oct), with no plate and the same 100 poses for every friction value (so its '1000 kits' were 100), said 98.1 % at 2 mm off for the 16 mm slot and 14 mm pockets; a fresh pose for every kit gave 96.7 % (`results/chute_dynamics_no_plate.txt`) and the pocket walls took it to 82.8 % (`results/chute_dynamics_8oct_14_16_18.txt`): that is why the numbers were raised. If the bench test shows bouncy kits, a 1 mm pad of TPU or felt on the channel's floor slab under the slot takes the bounce out (the hopper's own floor strip covers only 6 % of the void) **[proposal, not simulated]**.
- **What failed first and what was tried** (the first design; `v3-checks/results/chute_dynamics_first_design.txt`, `chute_dynamics_first_design_bore147.txt`): the channel runs 32.8 degrees off the sides of the 45-degree slot square, so a kit that falls straight down arrives turned 32.8 degrees to it. A 10.3 mm cube turned by θ is 10.3 (cos θ + sin θ) wide, 14.2 mm at 32.8 degrees, and the 13 mm bore takes it only within 18.2 degrees of its axis. Kits within 10 degrees of the slot square, which is how a kit sits against a pocket wall, did not get out in any of 10 trials at any friction value (they stopped in the hopper mouth on the low side walls of the trough cut flat at z 7.7); kits turned 20 degrees or more got out 10 of 10. It was geometry, not friction. **Widening the bore to 14.7 mm looked like the fix and was not:** a cube fits at any turn, yet the aligned kits rose only to 30 %. Four things had to change together: (1) the trough is cut down to the bore floor, not flat at z 7.7, which left low side walls for kits to rest on; (2) the slot and hopper void must be wider than the cube's face diagonal (14.5 mm is narrower than 14.57: a tipping kit wedges across the diagonal); (3) the bore must be wider than the space diagonal both ways (with bouncy impacts a 16 mm bore still wedged tumbling kits); (4) no wall end or loose fragment may stand where the trough meets the bore. Not taken: a guide in the hopper that turns the falling kit (an extra part, not simulated), and slot positions at 114.9 degrees on the ring so that the channel runs radially from the plate (it still jammed at friction 0.35 to 0.45 in a quick test, and eight pockets at the current 30-degree pitch do not fit between slots 130 degrees apart: the pitch would have to be 27 degrees, and the drop-sequence check `plate_variants` assumes slots 90 degrees apart).
- **Joint:** the channel slides in from outside through a matching hole in the wall (cut from the channel's own profile, so it is longer along the wall) and past the hopper's socket (snap detent). The exit is trimmed flush with the body radius. The first proposal was to print it in two halves lying flat (trough and cover), no supports; that split has not been redrawn for the open trough **[proposal]**.

### 8.5 Wi-Fi and Bluetooth

The GIGA R1 WiFi carries a Murata 1DX module: Wi-Fi 802.11 b/g/n (65 Mbps) as access point, station or both at once, and Bluetooth Low Energy **[src]** datasheet ABX00063 (sections 2 and 8). There is no on-board antenna. The module uses the u.FL socket J14, which sits at the front-inner corner of the board beside the reset button (about (7.2, -1.9), read from the datasheet picture, +-1 mm), and a flat flex antenna comes in the box (the datasheet lists "Micro UFL antenna (Included)"; its ISED section names a Molex 206994-0100 FPC antenna; a shop listing gives about 15.4 x 6.4 mm on a 100 mm cable **[src, secondary]** [Molex](https://www.molex.com/en-us/products/part-detail/2069940100)).

- **Antenna position [proposal]:** stuck on the inside of the thickened wall behind the right bumper plate (the recess wall, inner face r 10.15) at **-22 degrees** (x 9.4, y -3.8), strip vertical at z 5.0 to 6.54: plastic all round, 8.7 mm from the bumper's microswitch, 6.1 mm under the USB-C socket, 25 mm from the aluminium omni wheel over its whole travel, at least 3 mm from the boss rib at -12 degrees, and about 3 cm from J14, so the routed run (about 7 cm) fits the 100 mm cable **[calc]** unit test `test_wifi_antenna_is_stuck_on_the_bumper_recess_wall...`, Fusion `clearances`. **It moved on 9 Oct:** the 25.6 mm aluminium wheel was 2.8 mm from the first place (-6 degrees), and a metal body within a few millimetres detunes a 2.4 GHz antenna; the next place tried (-17.5 degrees at z 5.5 to 7.04) lay inside the bumper's recess wall and overlapped the tub by 0.036 cm3, which the emulator's dry run found. It stays with the tub, so taking the frame off does not unplug it. Not RF-tested: before the final print, check the range with the robot assembled (laptop and phone at 2 m and 5 m).
- **Use [proposal]:** a **test mode** chosen at power-up (for example the start button held while the power switch is turned on, with an LED showing that the radio is on), in which the firmware starts the radio, streams its log to a laptop (TCP, or Bluetooth serial for a phone) and accepts test commands. In the normal mode the radio is never started. **Rules 4.1.1:** "passing information ... wirelessly ... to the robot is not allowed" during a run, so the competition build receives nothing and the referee can be shown that. Do the final validation runs in the normal mode: the Wi-Fi stack changes timing.
- **Flashing:** over the USB-C socket in the wall, lid on. Wireless upload is possible with the `Arduino_Portenta_OTA` library but needs a one-time partitioning of the board's flash and has rough edges in the forums; the usual `ArduinoOTA` is reported not to support the GIGA. Treat it as an experiment **[src]** [Arduino support](https://support.arduino.cc/hc/en-us/articles/12370721200540-Configure-GIGA-R1-WiFi-Portenta-H7-and-Portenta-Machine-Control-for-Over-The-Air-OTA-uploads), [forum](https://forum.arduino.cc/t/arduinoota-on-giga-r1/1348426).
- **Venue:** 2.4 GHz is crowded at a competition; test mode is for the pit and the practice field.

### 8.6 IMU

The BNO055 breakout (Adafruit 2472: 20 x 27 mm board, 4 mm with the header, four mounting holes 20 mm apart along the board and 12 mm across **[src]** product page; **[measure]** on your board) sits on **four 5 mm posts on the tub floor at (-5.0, 0)**, header edge forward, held by four M2.5 self-tapping screws into 2 mm pilots **[proposal]**. The place is the free column behind the N20: 11.5 mm behind and below the N20 gearmotor and 25.6 mm under hopper A, with the hoppers and chutes beside it and nothing under the rear spoke **[check]** Fusion `clearances`. It is on the centre line, flat, so its pitch and roll are the robot's. The yaw rate does not depend on where the board is; the magnetometer does (the drive motors, the battery and the N20 are magnets and carry currents), so the firmware should run the chip in **IMU mode** (no magnetometer) until it is calibrated in place **[proposal]**: the V2 robot's loss of heading after a pause was traced to the magnetometer-fused mode (NDOF), which re-references itself when the robot is lifted. The chip's own position and the header are not modelled.

**Bench:** three motor cradles; the chute channel with 20 cubes at 32 degrees; the plate parked 0.5, 1, 1.5 and 2 mm off with kits at several turns, and the plate's parking repeatability from both sides; a printed bore against 10.5 mm kits; the kit's slide-off angle on printed PETG along the layer direction (friction) and its rebound; a weighted kit with an off-centre centre of mass; the seam of the two-half channel and the step where the hopper meets the floor slab; lid hooks; hopper seal; battery tray fit; wheel hub on the D-shaft; 5 kg hang from the handle; frame screw pull-out in PETG; N20 against 28BYJ-48 (rev 3 test 7); one ToF pocket printed and a board fitted (version, screws, plug); the six camera readings of Figure 11 and a test fit of one cage; the IMU in IMU mode (heading after a pick-up); the omni mount (backlog section 5): spring force against travel at the axle, spring life (10 000 cycles), axle-thread pull-out, the seat pin's fit in the arm, the slot end after the first landings (dent), ride height, the PETG arm fitted first, then the stair test.
